[ SIGNAL SCOUT / ROBOTICS / NETWORKING / MAPPING ]

Wi-Fi
Mapping Drone

A ~650 g four-rotor survey drone that pairs Wi-Fi signal readings with outdoor or indoor position data to produce a coverage heatmap.

JUMP TO: OVERVIEW / METHOD / STRUCTURE / VERIFICATION / DRAWINGS

01. Overview

Wi-Fi is invisible, which makes poor coverage difficult to diagnose. This project gives the network a physical survey tool: a small drone that can move through an environment and collect signal measurements from multiple locations.

The recorded values are plotted as a heatmap. Strong and weak zones become easy to compare, helping reveal dead spots and areas where the network may need improvement.

02. How it works

Four ducted rotors lift the ~650 g airframe. The flight controller keeps it stable and, in survey mode, moves it slowly between waypoints while the payload records Wi-Fi strength.

  1. STEP 01 Scan Fly a controlled path through the survey area.
  2. STEP 02 Record Pair each RSSI reading with position and time.
  3. STEP 03 Visualize Plot the microSD log as a coverage heatmap.

POSITION SYSTEM

Two ways to know where it is

OUTDOORS

GPS

A 25 × 25 mm GPS module sits at the nose on a 42 mm pedestal, giving the flight controller position when the drone has a clear sky view.

INDOORS

Optical flow + ToF

A downward optical-flow camera reads floor texture moving past to estimate velocity. A time-of-flight rangefinder measures height, replacing GPS where there is no sky view.

THE PAYLOAD CHAIN

From radio signal to heatmap

  1. Omnidirectional antenna

    A 60 mm non-conductive mast at the aft end picks up ambient Wi-Fi from every direction.

  2. RG178 coax

    The cable runs inside the hollow mast and carries the received radio signal down into the electronics pod.

  3. Receiver board

    The board measures RSSI for every beacon it hears. It never decodes network traffic; it only measures signal strength.

  4. Position + signal merge

    The pod pairs each RSSI value with the position reported by the flight controller—GPS outdoors or optical flow plus ToF indoors—and adds a timestamp.

  5. microSD log

    Each combined sample is written as one line to the card. After landing, those lines are plotted by location to create the heatmap.

KEY DESIGN POINT

The receiver never knows position, and the flight controller never knows signal strength. The board inside the pod is the only place where those two data streams meet.

03. Structure

The airframe is a bottom-to-top sandwich built around one structural carbon plate.

  1. Landing skids

    Touch down first and hold the frame 53 mm above the ground, leaving 10 mm of clearance below the battery.

  2. Battery tray

    Hangs below the plate on slotted bolts. Sliding the tray forward or aft is the center-of-gravity trim adjustment.

  3. FRAME_BOTTOM_PLATE

    The 3 mm carbon plate is the real airframe. Motors, ducts, skids, battery tray, mast, and sensor mounts bolt directly to it with no structural joints.

  4. FRAME_TOP_DECK

    Six standoffs raise a 2 mm carbon deck, forming a bay for the flight controller and ESCs. A printed chassis shell covers the bay but carries no structural load.

  5. Payload pod

    Bolts to the same four standoffs as the deck. Two plates and grommets isolate it from arm vibration so the propellers do not disturb the antenna or electronics.

CONTACT PROTECTION

Replaceable duct and bumper

Each duct has a 2.4 mm shell, Ø94 mm bore, and Ø104 mm outside diameter. A TPU 95A bumper clips around the outboard 160° and stands 2.2 mm proud, creating a cheap replaceable fuse before an impact reaches the carbon arm or motor.

POWER + BALANCE

Sliding battery tray

The 26 mm tray travel trims the center of gravity for different packs. The 649.3 g design point uses a 4S 1100 mAh, 128 g battery; filling the complete 85 × 40 × 38 mm envelope with a ~215 g pack would raise the aircraft to about 736 g.

SERVICEABLE POD

One removable payload

The 78 × 55 × 30 mm pod provides a 73.2 × 45.2 × 25 mm cavity with USB-C access, a dedicated microSD slot, a status-LED window, and baffled vents that keep prop wash from blowing directly onto the board.

MASS TRADEOFF

The structure accepts the larger battery envelope, but the sub-650 g target requires the lighter 4S 1100 mAh design-point pack.

ANTENNA OFFSET

The mast sits 53 mm aft of center to avoid the pod lid and propeller sweeps. Its fixed offset is entered once in the survey software.

CLEARANCE LIMIT

The Ø90 propeller inside the Ø94 duct leaves exactly 2.00 mm radial clearance. A stiff three-blade propeller is required.
