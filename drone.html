[ SIGNAL SCOUT / ROBOTICS / NETWORKING / MAPPING ]

Wi-Fi
Mapping Drone

A ~650 g four-rotor survey drone that pairs Wi-Fi signal readings with outdoor or indoor position data to produce a coverage heatmap.

JUMP TO: OVERVIEW / METHOD / STRUCTURE / FLIGHT HARDWARE / WIRING / ASSEMBLY / CHECKS / DRAWINGS

01. Overview

Wi-Fi is invisible, which makes poor coverage difficult to diagnose. This project gives the network a physical survey tool: a small drone that can move through an environment and collect signal measurements from multiple locations.

The recorded values are plotted as a heatmap. Strong and weak zones become easy to compare, helping reveal dead spots and areas where the network may need improvement.

02. How it works

Four ducted rotors lift the ~650 g airframe. The flight controller keeps it stable and, in survey mode, moves it slowly between waypoints while the payload records Wi-Fi strength.

  1. STEP 01 Scan Fly a controlled path through the survey area.
  2. STEP 02 Record Pair each RSSI reading with position and time.
  3. STEP 03 Visualize Plot the microSD log as a coverage heatmap.

POSITION SYSTEM

Two ways to know where it is

OUTDOORS

GPS

A 25 × 25 mm GPS module sits at the nose on a 42 mm pedestal, giving the flight controller position when the drone has a clear sky view.

INDOORS

Optical flow + ToF

A downward optical-flow camera reads floor texture moving past to estimate velocity. A time-of-flight rangefinder measures height, replacing GPS where there is no sky view.

THE PAYLOAD CHAIN

From radio signal to heatmap

  1. Omnidirectional antenna

    A 60 mm non-conductive mast at the aft end picks up ambient Wi-Fi from every direction.

  2. RG178 coax

    The cable runs inside the hollow mast and carries the received radio signal down into the electronics pod.

  3. Receiver board

    The board measures RSSI for every beacon it hears. It never decodes network traffic; it only measures signal strength.

  4. Position + signal merge

    The pod pairs each RSSI value with the position reported by the flight controller—GPS outdoors or optical flow plus ToF indoors—and adds a timestamp.

  5. microSD log

    Each combined sample is written as one line to the card. After landing, those lines are plotted by location to create the heatmap.

KEY DESIGN POINT

The receiver never knows position, and the flight controller never knows signal strength. The board inside the pod is the only place where those two data streams meet.

03. Structure

The airframe is a bottom-to-top sandwich built around one structural carbon plate.

  1. Landing skids

    Touch down first and hold the frame 53 mm above the ground, leaving 10 mm of clearance below the battery.

  2. Battery tray

    Hangs below the plate on slotted bolts. Sliding the tray forward or aft is the center-of-gravity trim adjustment.

  3. FRAME_BOTTOM_PLATE

    The 3 mm carbon plate is the real airframe. Motors, ducts, skids, battery tray, mast, and sensor mounts bolt directly to it with no structural joints.

  4. FRAME_TOP_DECK

    Six standoffs raise a 2 mm carbon deck, forming a bay for the flight controller and ESCs. A printed chassis shell covers the bay but carries no structural load.

  5. Payload pod

    Bolts to the same four standoffs as the deck. Two plates and grommets isolate it from arm vibration so the propellers do not disturb the antenna or electronics.

CONTACT PROTECTION

Replaceable duct and bumper

Each duct has a 2.4 mm shell, Ø94 mm bore, and Ø104 mm outside diameter. A TPU 95A bumper clips around the outboard 160° and stands 2.2 mm proud, creating a cheap replaceable fuse before an impact reaches the carbon arm or motor.

POWER + BALANCE

Sliding battery tray

The 26 mm tray travel trims the center of gravity for different packs. The 649.3 g design point uses a 4S 1100 mAh, 128 g battery; filling the complete 85 × 40 × 38 mm envelope with a ~215 g pack would raise the aircraft to about 736 g.

SERVICEABLE POD

One removable payload

The 78 × 55 × 30 mm pod provides a 73.2 × 45.2 × 25 mm cavity with USB-C access, a dedicated microSD slot, a status-LED window, and baffled vents that keep prop wash from blowing directly onto the board.

04. Lift and flight hardware

The aircraft is sized for slow survey flight, stable hovering, and useful control authority rather than speed. Four 1507/1806-class motors driving 90 mm three-blade propellers provide an estimated 2.2-2.6 thrust-to-weight ratio at the 649.3 g design mass.

MOTOR PLATFORM

One plate, two bolt patterns

Each motor sits on a 28 mm diameter arm pad. The lower carbon plate accepts both 16 × 16 mm and 19 × 19 mm M3 patterns, allowing a 1507/1806 motor or a larger 2204/2205 motor without cutting a new frame.

ELECTRONICS BAY

Flight stack on grommets

The 19 mm-high center bay accepts 30.5 × 30.5 mm and 20 × 20 mm flight-controller patterns. The controller and four-in-one ESC sit on rubber grommets between the 3 mm lower plate and 2 mm upper deck.

IMPACT LOAD PATH

A replaceable mechanical fuse

During a wall strike, the TPU bumper compresses first, the printed duct ring flexes second, and the four-bolt saddle carries what remains. The inexpensive duct is intended to fail before the carbon arm or motor bell.

CLEARANCE LIMIT

Exactly 2.00 mm radial

A 94 mm duct bore around a 90 mm propeller leaves no spare margin beyond the 2 mm requirement. A stiff three-blade propeller is required, and tip clearance must be checked again after a hard landing.

05. Wiring and data path

Cable paths are built into the model so power wiring, navigation sensors, and the radio measurement chain stay separated and remain serviceable.

MOTOR PHASES
Drop through an 8 × 4 mm slot beside each motor pad, run under the carbon arm in printed clips, then enter the ESC bay through a 7 mm grommeted port.
BATTERY LEAD
Runs aft through a 10 × 12 mm chassis opening to the XT30 connector, with its service loop held under the aft spine.
ANTENNA COAX
RG178 travels down the hollow mast, through a 2 mm strain-relief channel and service-loop pocket, then enters the pod at x = -34 mm on the side opposite the USB-C opening.
GPS
The module cable runs inside the hollow nose pedestal and passes through the upper deck slot at x = +34 mm.
FLOW + ToF
The two downward sensors share a loom along the nose-carrier webs and enter through the forward deck slot.

ONE LOGGED SAMPLE

TIME | BEACON_ID | RSSI_DBM | X | Y | Z | POSITION_SOURCE

This is the information joined inside the payload pod: one signal-strength reading, one reported position, and one timestamp. The microSD file is converted into a heatmap only after landing.

06. Build and service sequence

The assembly order keeps every fastener accessible and lets the ducts, skids, battery tray, and payload pod be replaced independently.

  1. Prepare the carbon

    Cut the 3 mm lower plate and 2 mm upper deck from the DXF profiles. Deburr, seal every edge, and add a 0.3 mm chamfer to both faces of each hole.

  2. Install inserts

    Fit 16 M3 heat-set inserts in the four duct saddles, eight M3 inserts in the flight-controller bosses, and 12 M2.5 inserts in the payload pod.

  3. Mount motors

    Fasten each motor from below with four M3 × 8 screws and route the phase wires before the ducts close access around the arms.

  4. Clamp ducts

    Place each saddle above its arm, the clamp below, and drive four M3 × 16 screws upward. Snap the TPU bumper into the outer flange groove.

  5. Add landing gear

    Fasten each skid leg to the arm with two M3 × 12 screws and nyloc nuts. Each skid remains independently replaceable.

  6. Build the center bay

    Set the printed chassis shell over the plate, add six 19 mm standoffs, install the ESC and flight controller on grommets, then fit the upper deck.

  7. Route the antenna

    Feed the RG178 through the mast before bolting its base to the aft spine, then close the strain-relief clamp around the cable.

  8. Fit navigation sensors

    Install the GPS pedestal above the nose and the optical-flow plus downward-ToF carrier below the forward spine.

  9. Close the payload

    Install the receiver, regulator, and microSD module in the pod. Bolt the pod to four deck standoffs and attach its lid last.

  10. Trim the balance

    Hang the battery tray below the frame, fit a live pack, slide the tray within its 26 mm range, and confirm the center of gravity on both arm axes.
